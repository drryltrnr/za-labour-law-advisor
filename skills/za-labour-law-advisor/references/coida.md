# COIDA (SKILL.md Section 2.B)

**Load when:** A workplace injury or occupational disease; any dealing with the Compensation Fund or a licensee (e.g. RMA); a compensation clawback; rehabilitation, return to work or an incapacity process after an injury.

Section numbers are the skill's section identifiers; SKILL.md Section 0.C shows which file holds each. This file records the law as last verified (`watch-list.md` lists what moves on known dates). Run SKILL.md Section 1 on everything in it before relying on it.

## B. COIDA 130 of 1993, as amended by Act 10 of 2022

**Commencement matters.** Practitioner commentary (Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, 12 March 2026; Webber Wentzel) reports that **Proclamation Notice 306 of 2026**, published on 23 January 2026, commenced the Amendment Act in three tranches: **23 January 2026** (most provisions), **1 February 2026** (the provisions reconstituting the Compensation Board) and **1 April 2026** (assessment payment deadlines, interest and penalty enforcement). The same commentary reports that **s 1(g)** of the Amendment Act — the substituted definition of "earnings" — and part of s 1(h) were held back. Open the proclamation itself and confirm its gazette number before citing it, and check for a later proclamation commencing s 1(g). Check the date of the conduct and of any document against the relevant tranche before choosing between the old and new text, and before characterising a request as a criminal referral rather than an administrative penalty.

**The 2026 Regulations.** Regulations under the amended Act were published on 6 March 2026, effective on publication (commentary cites Government Gazette No. 54273 — confirm the gazette and notice numbers before citing). As reported, they cover the transitional reach of the three-year claim period (s 43 below), inspection procedure (generally 14 days' notice to the employer before a compliance inspection, and 14 days for written representations in an investigation), rehabilitation and return to work (below), employer record-keeping, and registration of third parties. Read the regulation, not the summary, before relying on any period in it.

* **Right to compensation** — s 22 read with s 47. The 75% temporary total disablement rate is set by **s 47(1)(a) read with Schedule 4, Item 1**. Cite that pairing, never s 63, when quantifying compensation. Schedule 4's maximum and minimum amounts are amended by ministerial notice — fetch the operative notice per Section 1.E and check the claim against both. **Never call 75% of earnings a "floor":** it is the compensation payable, and the only floor in Item 1 is the gazetted minimum; an employer will exploit the confusion. Notice 7349 of 2026 states the Item 1 calculation as *"75% x monthly earnings at the time of the accident x number of days off/total days in month"* — cite that wording for any part-month.
* **s 22(2)** — *"No periodical payments shall be made in respect of temporary total disablement or temporary partial disablement which lasts for three days or less."* This is a threshold filter excluding de minimis injuries, not a waiting period: once disablement exceeds three days, compensation runs from the first day of disablement.
* **s 63 — "Manner of calculating earnings".** It governs only how the earnings base is computed (reference period, short-service employees, multi-employer cases, weekly-to-monthly conversion). It does not set compensation rates and houses no objections process. Say "compensation is calculated upon earnings determined under s 63", never "the rate under s 63". Verify the actual objections and appeal mechanism rather than assuming a section number for it.
* **Which definition of "earnings".** The Amendment Act substitutes the s 1 definition of "earnings" (Amendment Act s 1(g)), but that paragraph was reported as held back from the 2026 commencement. Until a proclamation commences it, compute earnings under the principal Act's definition read with s 63 — and check for that proclamation every time.
* **s 63(1) — what counts as earnings.** Earnings are calculated *"in such manner as in his opinion is best to determine the monthly rate at which the employee was being remunerated by his employer at the time of the accident"*, including under para (b) *"any overtime payment or other special remuneration in cash or in kind of a regular nature or for work ordinarily performed"*, but excluding *"(i) payment for intermittent overtime; (ii) payment for non-recurrent occasional services; (iii) amounts paid by an employer to an employee to cover any special expenses; (iv) ex gratia payments whether by the employer or any other person"*. Count the months: overtime paid in every month of the reference period is regular, not intermittent — put the month-by-month figures in the document (RMA's RMD01 itself lists overtime among the variable earnings to be averaged). Read the employer's accident report for the earnings it declared: a determination made on blank or R0.00 earnings fields is a ground to ask the insurer to re-determine, with any objection lodged in time on the prescribed form. **Where a component's inclusion is genuinely contestable** (leave pay, a once-off bonus), plead the primary figure and a fully computed alternative, and ask for the order in whichever amount follows from the earnings as determined — an all-or-nothing figure invites rejection of the whole quantum.
* **Employer's report of an accident — SEVEN days, not thirty.** **s 39(1)** requires the employer, within **seven days** of receiving notice of an accident or otherwise learning of it, to report it to the Commissioner **in the prescribed manner** (the W.Cl.2 under the Compensation Fund; an employer insured with a licensed mutual association reports on that insurer's prescribed form, for example RMA's RMD01). Two penalties attach, both substituted by s 19 of the Amendment Act: **s 39(6)** — an employer (other than one referred to in s 84(1)(a)(i)–(iii)) failing to comply with s 39(1) is liable to a penalty of **10% of the actual or estimated annual earnings** for that year (earnings, not payroll or turnover), replacing the former criminal offence; and **s 39(8)** — where such an employer fails to report in the prescribed manner within seven days, the Commissioner may impose a penalty equal to **the full amount of compensation payable, plus interest from the date of the accident**. Before alleging late reporting, establish the *actual* submission date from the insurer's or Fund's file: a portal export or print date on a copy of the form is not a submission date, and an unprovable allegation costs more than the point is worth. Where the date is unknown, ask the Inspector to compel the unredacted electronic claim file under s 93A / s 93D rather than asserting the breach.
* **Don't conflate the three time-lines.** **s 38(1)** — the *employee* gives written or verbal notice to the employer as soon as possible after the accident (and failure does not bar the right to compensation where the employer knew from another source, s 38(2)). **s 39(1)** — the *employer* reports to the Commissioner within seven days. **s 43** — the *claim* is lodged with the Commissioner, employer or mutual association within **three years** of the accident under the Act as amended; the principal Act's period was **12 months**. Three practitioner sources (Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, Webber Wentzel ×2) report the extension, and report that the 2026 Regulations apply it to accidents that occurred before commencement on 23 January 2026. Read s 43 as substituted, and the transitional regulation, before stating the period for any accident — and never state 12 months without first establishing which regime governs that accident. Under the principal Act's s 43(1)(b) a late claim was not considered except where the accident was reported under s 39; check whether the substituted section keeps that exception.
* **s 32 and s 33 (the ring-fence).** s 32(1) provides that, *notwithstanding anything to the contrary in any other law contained*, compensation shall not — (a) be ceded or pledged; (b) be capable of attachment or any form of execution under a judgment or order of a court of law; (d) be set off against any debt of the person entitled to the compensation. **s 32(1)(c), which provided that compensation shall not "be subject to income tax", was repealed by s 12 of Act 61 of 1997 — do not cite it.** The tax exemption now lives in Income Tax Act s 10(1)(gB)(i); s 32 says nothing about tax, so never describe it as barring "taxation" of compensation. s 33 voids any agreement provision by which an employee cedes or relinquishes benefits. Consent cures neither. Any clawback of money advanced as compensation is audited under s 32(1) first, with BCEA s 34 as the separate ordinary-wage analysis where what was taken is not the compensation itself. **Note what s 32 and s 33 do *not* say:** they are not a prohibition on deducting expenses from wages — that is s 64.
* **s 64 — narrow, and the most commonly mis-cited section in the Act.** It addresses an employer deducting from an employee's earnings to compensate *itself* for an amount **the employer** is liable to pay under the Act (its own assessments); the employer becomes liable to a penalty and the Commissioner can direct repayment. It is **not** a general bar on unlawful deductions, and it does **not** reach an employer clawing back an alleged over-payment of compensation it already paid the employee — that is the opposite direction and belongs under s 32(1) and/or BCEA s 34. Check which way the money moved before citing it.
* **s 47(3) — read each paragraph for what it actually says.**
  * **(a)** obliges *"the employer in whose service an employee is at the time of the accident"* to pay compensation for the first three months from the date of the accident.
  * **(b)**, as substituted, provides that *"After the expiry of the said three months, compensation so paid by such employer shall be repaid to the employer by the Compensation Commissioner or licensee concerned, as the case may be."* This is a **reimbursement-to-employer** provision. It does **not** transfer the paying obligation. After the three months the employer's duty under (a) simply ends, and liability for compensation rests with the Commissioner or licensee under the Act generally — do not cite (b) for a liability shift.
  * **(c)**, as substituted by s 28 of Act 10 of 2022: an employer who fails to comply with paragraph (a) *"shall be liable to a penalty equal to double the full amount of three months compensation payable plus interest"* — an administrative penalty, no longer an offence.
* **s 99, as substituted by s 61 of Act 10 of 2022** — *"Any person who does not comply with the provisions of sections 39, 40, 47, 64, 68, 81, 82 and 83 of this Act shall be liable to a penalty or penalties as specified in the said sections."* This is the general hook and it expressly lists ss 39 and 47, so "s 47(3)(c) read with s 99" and "s 39(6)/(8) read with s 99" are both correct.
* **Rehabilitation and reintegration** — the Amendment Act inserts a rehabilitation and reintegration framework (reported as Chapter VIIA, with a new s 70A) placing positive duties on the Compensation Fund, licensees and employers; commentary reports that an employer must use all available rehabilitation and reintegration processes before laying off an injured employee. As reported, the 2026 Regulations require an employer to appoint an employee health and wellness representative, include rehabilitation provisions in its HR policies, provide reasonable accommodation and transitional or temporary work, keep rehabilitation records for at least 30 years, and report the dismissal of an injured employee to the Chief Inspector. Read the inserted provisions and the regulations before relying on any of these. Address them before any incapacity dismissal is contemplated, and before advising an injured employee to resign (Section 3.B). Domestic workers are covered, with retrospective effect.
* **OHSA reporting runs alongside s 39.** The Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, s 24, imposes a separate duty to report certain incidents to a DEL inspector, in the manner and within the period its regulations prescribe. Read s 24 and the current regulations before alleging a breach; it is a second, independent compliance point, not a substitute for COIDA s 39.
* **Enforcement** — a **s 93F** compliance order may be sought from a DEL Inspector for a COIDA contravention; **s 93G** allows it to be made an order of court on non-compliance; **ss 93A and 93D** carry the inspection and production powers. **s 58** governs advances on compensation — reach for it when hardship gridlocks a claim. **s 56** allows application for increased compensation on the grounds of employer negligence.
* **Administrative channels** — RMA and other licensees for their sectors, otherwise Compensation Fund procedures.
