# Employment Equity Act (SKILL.md Section 2.D)

**Load when:** Unfair discrimination, equal pay for work of equal value, harassment.

Section numbers are the skill's section identifiers; SKILL.md Section 0.C shows which file holds each. This file records the law as last verified (`watch-list.md` lists what moves on known dates). Run SKILL.md Section 1 on everything in it before relying on it.

## D. EEA 55 of 1998

* **Unfair discrimination (s 6)** — no person may unfairly discriminate, directly or indirectly, against an employee in any employment policy or practice on a listed ground (race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, family responsibility, ethnic or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, HIV status, conscience, belief, political opinion, culture, language, birth) *"or on any other arbitrary ground"* (s 6(1), as amended in 2013). Affirmative action consistent with the Act and an inherent requirement of the job are not unfair discrimination (s 6(2)); harassment on a listed ground is (s 6(3)). Job applicants count as employees for ss 6–8 (s 9).
* **Equal pay (s 6(4)–(5), from 1 August 2014)** — a difference in terms and conditions between employees of the same employer doing the same, substantially the same or equal-value work, directly or indirectly based on a listed ground, is unfair discrimination (s 6(4)). The Minister may prescribe the criteria and methodology for assessing work of equal value (s 6(5)) — fetch the current regulations before pleading comparability.
* **Burden of proof (s 11, as substituted in 2013)** — on a listed ground, the employer must prove on a balance of probabilities that the discrimination did not take place as alleged, or is rational and not unfair, or is otherwise justifiable; on an arbitrary ground, the complainant must prove that the conduct is not rational, amounts to discrimination and is unfair. Choose and plead the ground accordingly.
* **Procedure (s 10)** — refer to the CCMA within six months of the act or omission (s 10(2)), condonable on good cause (s 10(3)); the referring party must show that the referral was served on every other party and that it made a reasonable attempt to resolve the dispute (s 10(4)). If conciliation fails: the Labour Court (s 10(6)(a)); CCMA arbitration where the employee alleges sexual harassment or, in any other case, earns below the BCEA s 6(3) threshold (s 10(6)(aA)), or where all parties consent (s 10(6)(b)). An award under (aA) may be appealed to the Labour Court within 14 days (s 10(8)). A discriminatory dismissal is not an s 10 dispute (s 10(1)): it goes under LRA s 187(1)(f) and s 191 (Section 2.C).
* **Employer liability for employees (s 60)** — conduct by an employee at work that would breach the Act must be brought to the employer's attention immediately; an employer that does not consult and take the necessary steps is deemed to have contravened the provision itself, unless it proves it did all that was reasonably practicable (s 60(1)–(4)). Report harassment to the employer in writing and keep proof of when.
* **Harassment Code** — the Code of Good Practice on the Prevention and Elimination of Harassment in the Workplace (GN R.1890, GG 46056 of 18 March 2022), issued under s 54(1)(b) and effective on publication, which repealed the 2005 sexual harassment code (GN 1357, GG 27865). Apply the code in force when the conduct occurred.
* **Amendment Act 4 of 2022** — in force 1 January 2025 (Proclamation Notice 231 of 2024, GG 51684). It deletes the turnover limb of "designated employer" and repeals Schedule 4, so an employer with fewer than 50 employees is no longer designated by turnover; s 15A lets the Minister set sectoral numerical targets by Gazette notice; s 53(6) conditions state-contract compliance certificates on, among other things, no CCMA or court finding of unfair discrimination in the previous 12 months and no CCMA award for failing to pay the minimum wage. Fetch the current s 15A target notices.
