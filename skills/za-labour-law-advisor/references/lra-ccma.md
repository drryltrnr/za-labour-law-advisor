# LRA and CCMA Practice (SKILL.md Section 2.C)

**Load when:** Any dismissal, retrenchment, unfair labour practice, referral, conciliation, arbitration, award enforcement, rescission or review; labour-broker (TES) placements.

Section numbers are the skill's section identifiers; SKILL.md Section 0.C shows which file holds each. This file records the law as last verified (`watch-list.md` lists what moves on known dates). Run SKILL.md Section 1 on everything in it before relying on it.

## C. LRA 66 of 1995 and CCMA practice

* **Binding and enforcement architecture** — **s 31** (agreement binds the parties and their members); **s 32** (extension to non-parties by ministerial notice); **s 32A** (renewal of funding agreements for up to twelve months); **s 33A** (council enforcement: designated-agent compliance orders, arbitration, an arbitrator empowered to order payment and impose a fine, awards final, binding and enforceable). Identify which of these a given obligation rests on before pleading it.
* **What counts as a dismissal (s 186(1))** — the employer terminating employment with or without notice; not renewing a fixed-term contract, or renewing it on less favourable terms, where the employee reasonably expected renewal — or, from 1 January 2015, reasonably expected to be kept on indefinitely on the same or similar terms (s 186(1)(b)(ii)); refusing to let an employee resume work after maternity leave; re-employing some but not others dismissed for the same reason; constructive dismissal (s 186(1)(e), Section 3.C); leaving because the new employer made conditions substantially less favourable after a s 197 or s 197A transfer (s 186(1)(f)). The employee must establish the dismissal; the employer must then prove it fair (s 192).
* **Automatically unfair dismissals (s 187(1))** — among others, dismissal for taking part in a protected strike or protest action; refusing to do the work of strikers or locked-out employees; *"a refusal by employees to accept a demand in respect of any matter of mutual interest between them and their employer"* (s 187(1)(c), as substituted in 2014); exercising rights or taking part in proceedings under the LRA; pregnancy; unfair discrimination on a listed or any arbitrary ground; a s 197 or s 197A transfer; a protected disclosure. s 187(2) saves an inherent requirement of the job and dismissal at the normal or agreed retirement age. These go to the Labour Court (s 191(5)(b)(i)), with compensation up to 24 months' remuneration (s 194(3)).
* **Every other dismissal (s 188)** — unfair unless the employer proves a fair reason related to conduct, capacity or its operational requirements, and a fair procedure. Anyone deciding fairness *"must take into account any relevant code of good practice issued in terms of this Act"* (s 188(2)).
* **Code of Good Practice: Dismissal** — GN 3470, GG 53294 of 4 September 2025, issued under s 203(2A) (which s 203(2B) extends to replacing an existing code) and in force on publication. The notice states that it repeals Schedule 8 and the 1999 Code of Good Practice on Dismissal Based on Operational Requirements (GN 1517, GG 20254). Apply the Code in force at the date of dismissal — Schedule 8 for earlier dismissals. Misconduct: *"Generally, dismissal is only an appropriate sanction if a continued employment relationship is intolerable"* (item 9), tested against the rule-and-sanction checklist (item 8) and consistency (item 10). Procedure: notice of the allegations, preferably in writing; a reasonable time to prepare and answer on both the allegations and the sanction; a fellow employee or union representative; a language the employee is comfortable with, where reasonably possible (item 11(4)). Probation: items 14–18. Poor performance after probation: evaluation, instruction, training, guidance or counselling, and a reasonable period to improve (item 19). Ill health or injury: investigate, consider alternatives and adapted duties, with a *"more onerous"* duty to accommodate an employee injured at work or incapacitated by work-related illness (item 21(5)). Small employers get latitude (item 3). Retrenchment: items 22–24 and the Annexure A notice.
* **Retrenchment (ss 189, 189A)** — consult the parties in the order s 189(1) sets (collective agreement; then workplace forum and registered unions; then the affected employees or their nominated representatives) in *"a meaningful joint consensus-seeking process"* on avoiding, minimising, timing and mitigating the dismissals, the selection method and severance (s 189(2)). The written notice must disclose the s 189(3) list: reasons, alternatives considered and why each was rejected, numbers and job categories, selection method, timing, proposed severance, assistance offered, re-employment prospects, headcount, and retrenchments in the previous 12 months. The employer must respond to representations, in writing where they were made in writing (s 189(6)), and select by agreed criteria or, failing agreement, criteria that are *"fair and objective"* (s 189(7)). s 189A applies to employers of more than 50 employees at the s 189A(1) thresholds (from 10 dismissals for up to 200 employees to 50 for more than 500, counting the previous 12 months): a facilitator on request (s 189A(3)); where one is appointed, no notice of termination until 60 days after the s 189(3) notice (s 189A(7)); a consulting party may not unreasonably refuse to extend consultation (s 189A(2)(d), 2014). Procedural unfairness in a s 189A retrenchment is challenged only by application under s 189A(13), brought within 30 days of the notice of termination or of dismissal (s 189A(17)); the Labour Court may not decide procedural fairness in the substantive referral (s 189A(18)). Minimum severance: BCEA s 41.
* **Where a dismissal dispute goes (s 191)** — refer within 30 days of the dismissal or, if later, of the final decision to dismiss or uphold the dismissal, to the council with jurisdiction, otherwise the CCMA (s 191(1)); an employee dismissed on notice may refer once notice is received (s 191(2A)). If conciliation fails, or 30 days (or a longer agreed period) pass: arbitration for conduct or capacity dismissals, constructive dismissal and s 186(1)(f) claims (unless a s 187 reason is alleged), an unknown reason, and unfair labour practices (s 191(5)(a)); the Labour Court for automatically unfair dismissals, retrenchments, dismissals for an unprotected strike and closed-shop dismissals (s 191(5)(b)), referred within 90 days of the certificate (s 191(11)). Probation disputes — and any other s 191(5)(a) dispute unless a party objects — go straight to arbitration once the certificate issues (s 191(5A)). A retrenched employee may choose arbitration or the Labour Court where the consultation covered that employee only, only that employee was dismissed, or the employer has fewer than ten employees (s 191(12), as substituted in 2014).
* **Date of dismissal (s 190)** — the earlier of the date the contract ended and the date the employee left service (s 190(1)); where the employer terminates on notice, the date the notice expires or, if earlier, the date all outstanding salary is paid (s 190(2)(d), from 1 January 2015). The 30-day clock runs from this date.
* **Remedies (ss 193–195)** — reinstatement or re-employment is the default: it must be ordered unless the employee does not want it, the relationship would be intolerable, it is not reasonably practicable, or the dismissal is unfair only on procedure (s 193(2)). Compensation must be *"just and equitable in all the circumstances"*, capped at 12 months' remuneration at the rate on the date of dismissal (s 194(1)) or 24 months for an automatically unfair dismissal (s 194(3)), and it is in addition to any other amount owed under a law, collective agreement or contract (s 195) — claim notice, leave and severance pay separately.
* **ss 198–198D (TES)** — a TES employee placed with a client, earning below the BCEA threshold and not performing a "temporary service" as s 198A(1) defines it (in general, beyond three months) is deemed the client's employee for the purposes of the LRA (s 198A(3)(b)). The Constitutional Court read this as making the client the sole employer for LRA purposes in *Assign Services (Pty) Ltd v NUMSA* [2018] ZACC 22 (26 July 2018) — open it before citing. Joint and several liability is a different rule in a different section: the TES and the client are jointly and severally liable for the contraventions listed in s 198(4), and s 198(4A) lets the employee institute proceedings against either or both. Cite the section that matches the relief sought.
* **Unfair labour practices (s 186(2))** — the definition covers four things only: unfair conduct relating to promotion, demotion, probation (not probation dismissals), training or the provision of benefits; unfair suspension or other disciplinary action short of dismissal; a failure or refusal to reinstate or re-employ a former employee under an agreement; and an occupational detriment, other than dismissal, for making a protected disclosure. Refer within 90 days of the act or omission or, if later, of the date the employee became aware of it (s 191(1)(b)(ii)). Arbitration follows failed conciliation (s 191(5)(a)(iv)); probation disputes go straight to arbitration after the certificate (s 191(5A)(b)); a protected-disclosure detriment may go to the Labour Court instead (s 191(13)). The arbitrator may order reinstatement, re-employment or compensation on reasonable terms (s 193(4)), compensation being capped at 12 months' remuneration (s 194(4)). An unlawful deduction is not on the list on its face — claim it under BCEA s 34 (Section 2.A), and plead it as a "benefits" dispute only on current case law you have opened.
* **Referral timelines (s 191)** — dismissal, 30 days (above; bargaining council or CCMA, Form LRA 7.11; full constructive dismissal procedure in Section 3.C); unfair labour practice under s 186(2), 90 days; unfair discrimination under EEA s 10, 6 months. File condonation immediately where out of time, addressing degree of lateness, explanation, prospects of success and prejudice.
* **Conciliation, awards and what follows (ss 135, 143–145)** — the commissioner must attempt conciliation within 30 days of the referral, extendable by agreement, and then issue a certificate of outcome (s 135(2), (5)). An arbitration award is final and binding and, once the director has certified it, enforceable as if it were a Labour Court order in respect of which a writ has been issued (s 143(1), (3)); a money award carries interest at the prescribed rate from the date of the award unless it says otherwise (s 143(2)) and is executed as if it were a Magistrate's Court order (s 143(5)); a certified award ordering an act other than payment can be enforced by contempt proceedings in the Labour Court without further order (s 143(4)) — the 2014 versions of (1), (4) and (5) apply to awards issued from 1 January 2015 (s 143(6)). A commissioner may vary or rescind an award or ruling erroneously sought or made in a party's absence, one with an ambiguity or obvious error, one granted through a mistake common to the parties, or one made in a party's absence where good cause is shown (s 144, para (d) added in 2014). Review lies to the Labour Court within six weeks of service of the award (or of discovering corruption), for misconduct, gross irregularity, excess of powers or an improperly obtained award (s 145(1)–(2)); the applicant must apply for a hearing date within six months (s 145(5)); the review does not suspend the award unless the applicant furnishes security — 24 months' remuneration for a reinstatement or re-employment order, or the compensation awarded (s 145(7)–(8)); and it interrupts prescription on the award (s 145(9)). Subsections (5)–(8) apply to review applications brought, and (9) to awards issued, after 1 January 2015 (s 145(10)).
* **CCMA rules and practice directives** — check the current rules on electronic service, digital filing and representation before assuming a service method is good. The time limits for requesting arbitration, applying for rescission and seeking condonation are set by the CCMA Rules, not the Act — read the rule in force before stating a period.
* **Drafting standard** — referrals, representations and closing statements must be exhaustive, factually dense, chronologically sound and anchored in documents: call recordings, emails, medical certificates, payslips, portal exports.
