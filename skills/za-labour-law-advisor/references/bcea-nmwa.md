## A. BCEA 75 of 1997 and NMWA 9 of 2018

* **s 6(3) earnings threshold** — the Minister's s 6(3) determination lists what stops applying above the threshold. The 2025 and 2026 determinations (GN R.5970 of 7 March 2025; GN 7384 of 17 April 2026) both exclude ss 9–12 (ordinary hours, overtime, compressed week, averaging), ss 14–16 (meal intervals, rest periods, Sunday pay), s 17(2) (night-work conditions) and s 18(3) (pay for working a public holiday that is not an ordinary working day). Leave, public-holiday pay under s 18(2), notice and termination pay still apply above the threshold. "Earnings" in these determinations exclude subsistence and transport allowances, achievement awards and overtime pay. Separately, Chapter 2 (except s 7) does not apply at all to senior managerial employees, travelling sales staff who regulate their own hours, or anyone working under 24 hours a month for the employer (s 6(1)). The threshold also gates BCEA ss 9A, 70 and 73A. Fetch the current determination, its effective date and any correction notice per Section 1.E every time.
* **Hours, overtime, Sundays and public holidays** — ordinary hours may not exceed 45 a week, and 9 a day for a five-day week or 8 a day for a longer week (s 9(1)). Overtime needs an agreement and is capped at 3 hours a day and 10 a week (s 10(1)), paid at least one and a half times the wage (s 10(2)); an overtime agreement may not require more than 12 hours on any day (s 10(1A)), and a collective agreement may raise overtime to 15 hours a week for no more than two months in any 12 (s 10(6)). Sunday work is paid at double the wage, or one and a half times where the employee ordinarily works Sundays (s 16(1)). A public holiday falling on an ordinary working day is paid even if not worked, and working it earns at least double (s 18(2)). Below the threshold, an employee who works fewer than four hours on a day is paid for four (s 9A, inserted by Act 7 of 2018).
* **s 34 (deductions)** — a deduction is unlawful unless (i) the employee agreed in writing to the deduction in respect of a debt specified in the agreement, or (ii) the deduction is required or permitted by law, collective agreement, court order or arbitration award. **s 34(2)** sets the conditions for deductions in respect of loss or damage, including that the total of such deductions may not exceed one-quarter of the employee's remuneration in money. **s 34(5)** — *"An employer may not require or permit an employee to— (a) repay any remuneration except for overpayments previously made by the employer resulting from an error in calculating the employee's remuneration; or (b) acknowledge receipt of an amount greater than the remuneration actually received."* **Read s 34 in full before relying on the interplay between (1), (2) and (5).** The one-quarter cap in s 34(2)(d) governs loss-or-damage deductions only; separately, NMWA s 5(4) provides that *"a deduction made in terms of section 34(1)(a) of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act does not exceed one quarter of a worker's remuneration"* — check both before quantifying any recovery by deduction. Unilateral employer "clawbacks" of a disputed amount are self-help and unlawful — *North West Provincial Legislature v NEHAWU obo 158 Members* [2023] ZALAC 12 (21 June 2023). For the constitutional footing of the rule against an employer recovering a disputed sum by deduction without consent or a court order, see *Public Servants Association obo Ubogu v Head of the Department of Health, Gauteng* [2017] ZACC 45 (7 December 2017), which concerned the Public Service Act's recovery provision — open both and confirm the holding before citing either (Section 1.D). A clawback can never bypass a statutory calculation or breach a minimum wage.
* **Pay, payslips and fund contributions** — remuneration is paid daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly, no later than seven days after the pay period ends or the contract terminates (s 32(1), (3)), with a written statement each payday giving the s 33(1) particulars: employer and employee, period, remuneration, the amount and purpose of every deduction, the amount actually paid, and rates and hours where relevant. An employer that deducts a contribution to a pension, provident, retirement, medical aid or similar fund must pay it over within seven days of the deduction, and its own contributions within seven days of the end of the period (s 34A, inserted in 2002), unless the fund's rules require sooner — in any sector, alongside PFA s 13A (Section 2.F). **Labour inspectors now enforce s 34A for retirement funds.** For years a ministerial variation notice excluded Pension Funds Act funds from inspector enforcement of s 34A; the Minister withdrew it in January 2026 (commentary gives 8 and 13 January — fetch the withdrawal notice and cite its own date and number). Since then an inspector can secure an undertaking or issue a compliance order (ss 68–69) for unremitted retirement-fund contributions — a free route that runs beside the PFA s 13A and LRA s 33A routes. Note the timing difference: s 34A counts seven days from the *deduction*, PFA s 13A(1) from the end of the month; plead each on its own terms. The Employment Laws Amendment Bill, 2025 proposes new BCEA ss 62B and 77B empowering the Labour Court, CCMA and bargaining councils to order unpaid contributions paid to the fund with interest — it is a bill, not law, until passed and proclaimed (`watch-list.md`).
* **Leave (Chapter 3)** — annual: at least 21 consecutive days a cycle or, by agreement, a day for every 17 days worked (s 20(2)), granted within six months of the cycle's end (s 20(4)). Sick: six weeks' worth of paid sick leave in each 36-month cycle, and a day for every 26 days worked in the first six months (s 22); pay may be withheld for an absence of more than two consecutive days, or on more than two occasions in eight weeks, if a requested medical certificate is not produced (s 23(1)). Maternity: at least four consecutive months (s 25(1)); the BCEA does not make the employer pay it — benefits run under the UIA (s 25(7)). Parental leave of ten consecutive days, adoption leave of ten weeks for a child under two, and commissioning parental leave of ten weeks (ss 25A–25C, in force 1 January 2020, GN R.1699, GG 42925). These provisions were the subject of a Constitutional Court ruling in *Van Wyk v Minister of Employment and Labour*; read the order itself for the interim position, and check for amending legislation and its commencement — the Employment Laws Amendment Bill, 2025 (GN 3801, GG 54220, 26 February 2026) proposes substituting ss 25–25B and repealing s 25C, and is not law until passed and proclaimed. Family responsibility: three paid days a cycle for an employee with more than four months' service working at least four days a week, when their child is sick or on the death of a listed relative (s 27); the "child is born" ground was repealed from 1 January 2020.
* **Termination (Chapter 5)** — notice of at least one week for up to six months' service, two weeks from six months to a year, and four weeks after a year or, for farm and domestic workers, after six months (s 37(1), as amended in 2002); a collective agreement may cut the four weeks to no less than two (s 37(2)(b)). Notice must be in writing (s 37(4)), and an employer's notice may not be given during the employee's leave or run with it, except sick leave (s 37(5)); the employer may pay in lieu (s 38). On termination the employer must pay untaken time off, annual leave due and — after four months' employment — pro-rata leave for the incomplete cycle (s 40). Severance is at least one week's remuneration per completed year of continuous service on a dismissal for operational requirements or an insolvency termination (s 41(2), as amended in 2002), lost only by unreasonably refusing alternative employment (s 41(4)); a dispute only about severance goes to the council or CCMA, then arbitration (s 41(6)–(9)). The employee is entitled to a certificate of service with the s 42 particulars, including the reason for termination if the employee asks. Chapter 5 does not apply below 24 hours a month (s 36).
* **Which route for a money claim** — a labour inspector may secure an undertaking or issue a compliance order (ss 68–69), but not for an amount owed to an employee under the BCEA or NMWA where the employee earns above the threshold, where proceedings for that amount have been instituted in the CCMA or a court (unless withdrawn), or where it has been payable for more than 36 months before the complaint or the inspector's first step (s 70, as substituted by Act 7 of 2018). Below the threshold, an employee may refer a failure to pay any amount owing under the BCEA, the NMWA, the contract, a sectoral determination or a collective agreement to the CCMA (s 73A(1)–(2)); above it, the claim goes to the Labour Court, the High Court or, within their jurisdiction, the Magistrates' Court or small claims court (s 73A(3)). The Labour Court shares jurisdiction with the civil courts over any matter concerning a contract of employment (s 77(3)).
* **NMWA** — every worker is entitled to at least the national minimum wage (s 4(4)); it *"cannot be waived"* and overrides any contrary contract, collective agreement, sectoral determination or law (s 4(6)); a unilateral change to wages, hours or other conditions in connection with implementing it is an unfair labour practice (s 4(8), retrospective to 1 May 2017 under s 17(4) as corrected by Act 3 of 2020). The wage is the money paid for ordinary hours, excluding work-enabling allowances, payment in kind, and bonuses, tips and gifts (s 5(1)); pay by task or piece must still reach the minimum for ordinary hours (s 5(3)). Check whether the employer holds an exemption (s 15, at most one year). The Act does not cover the SANDF, NIA or SASS (s 3(1)). Fetch the current rate per Section 1.E.
