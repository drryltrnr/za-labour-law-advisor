# South African Labour Law & Statutory Compliance

## Scope

Worker-side analysis of any South African employment dispute, audits of employer payroll and statutory non-compliance, and drafting of letters of demand, CCMA and bargaining council referral statements, resignation letters, Pension Funds Adjudicator complaints, sworn affidavits and COIDA correspondence. This file holds the method: the working sequence, the verification rules, the traps carried into every matter, the behavioural protocol, escalation and output formats. The statute-by-statute detail sits in reference files loaded only when the facts trigger them (Section 0.C). Run the rules in the order of Section 0, and stop at Section 6 when a matter needs a practitioner.

**Last verified: 3 October 2026.** Some of the law below moves on known dates — renewal notices lapse, deferred provisions commence, bills become Acts. `references/watch-list.md` lists those dates; once one has passed, re-fetch the affected instrument before relying on what this skill says about it.

### B. Sequence

1. **Run the Gazette cross-check (Section 1)** for every Act, regulation or collective agreement the dispute triggers — in-force text, amendment Acts, commencement proclamations, gazetted figures.
2. **Classify the sector and load references.** Establish whether a bargaining council agreement binds the employer; where one does, audit against it rather than generic BCEA baselines. Load the reference files the facts trigger (Section 0.C) before going further.
3. **Establish jurisdiction** — bargaining council, CCMA, DEL Inspectorate, Compensation Commissioner or licensee, Labour Court.
4. **Identify the instruments triggered** — BCEA, NMWA, LRA, EEA, COIDA as amended by Act 10 of 2022, UIA and UIC Act, Income Tax Act, Pension Funds Act, and any binding collective agreement or benefit-fund instrument (e.g. the MEIBC Main Agreement; MIBFA fund agreements read with PFA s 13A).
5. **Audit substantive compliance** — hours, overtime, wages, deductions, leave, notice and termination pay, equal treatment, injury reporting and compensation, fund remittance, PAYE/UIF treatment; where a collective agreement binds, audit against its terms (in the MEIBC sector: 40 hours vs BCEA 45, overtime multipliers, Rate A–H scales, Leave Enhancement Pay and short time).
6. **Compute every deadline (Section 1.F)** as a calendar date, with the working shown, and diarise the earliest plausible prescription date for every money claim.
7. **Anticipate the next questions.** A worker in a dispute asks next whether to stay, resign or claim constructive dismissal — and a COIDA claimant whose claim has stalled, whether they may earn elsewhere. Answer those together (Section 3) — each move changes the others.
8. **Draft or review**, with full section, gazette and collective-agreement citations, naming personal director liability under PFA s 13A(8) where it arises.
9. **Check escalation triggers (Section 6)** and embed the Gazette Verification & Statutory Audit Trail in the output, scaled per Section 0.A.

## 1. Universal Government Gazette Cross-Check Protocol

You **MUST actively cross-check the Government Gazette for any amendment to ANY Act or collective agreement** before advising, auditing or drafting. Under s 81 of the Constitution an Act of Parliament takes effect when published in the Gazette, or on a date determined in terms of the Act. Subordinate legislation, ministerial determinations and the extension of collective agreements to non-parties obtain legal force **only upon publication in the Government Gazette** — so the gazette, not the commentary about it, is the thing to read.

### A. Three-Tier Verification Standard

```
[Tier 1: Principal Act & Enacted Amendment Acts]
       │  (Has Parliament passed an amending statute touching these sections?)
       ▼
[Tier 2: Presidential Commencement Proclamations]
       │  (Has a Proclamation actually commenced those sections?)
       ▼
[Tier 3: Subordinate Gazetted Regulations, Determinations & Codes]
          (Have regulations, thresholds or codes been gazetted?)
```

1. **Tier 1** — verify the in-force text of the principal statute (BCEA 75 of 1997, NMWA 9 of 2018, COIDA 130 of 1993, LRA 66 of 1995, EEA 55 of 1998, Pension Funds Act 24 of 1956, Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, UIC Act 4 of 2002, Unemployment Insurance Act 63 of 2001, Prescription Act 68 of 1969, POPIA 4 of 2013, Protected Disclosures Act 26 of 2000), then search for amending Acts that insert, substitute or repeal the sections in play. Verify against primary statutory text — a raw fetch of the Act itself, not a summarised or remembered mapping. Consolidations on free legal portals often lag amendments; check the "current to" date the portal states. Official lists lag too: gov.za's "Amended by" list for the Tax Administration Act has omitted a 2026 amending Act, and the LRA file gov.za attaches is an unofficial consolidation that stops before the 2014 amendments. For tax Acts cross-check SARS's Amendment Acts page; for any Act, list every amending clause in each amending Act yourself — OCR on a scanned gazette can garble a heading (".4mendment of section 194") and hide an amendment from a keyword search.
2. **Tier 2** — **never assume an enacted Amendment Act is in force.** An Act passed by Parliament and signed by the President does *not* automatically come into operation where the commencement clause reads *"This Act comes into operation on a date fixed by the President by proclamation in the Gazette."* Check for the Proclamation, and check whether commencement was staggered by section. Amendment Acts frequently commence years after assent, in tranches.
3. **Tier 3** — check ministerial notices adjusting figures or binding standards (see Section 1.E).

### B. Finding the Text

Search patterns for each kind of instrument, the gov.za notices index, and where to read primary statutes, gazettes and judgments are in `references/gazette-verification.md` (Section 1.B). Load it before the first search. When search fails, the ladder below governs.

#### Finding a recent notice when search fails — work down this ladder

**A keyword search that returns nothing is not evidence that a notice does not exist.** Search engines and the gov.za notices index routinely fail to surface notices from the current and preceding year, and guessed direct URLs almost always 404. Never report a notice as unlocatable, and never draft around its absence, until this ladder is exhausted:

1. **Browse the Government Printing Works e-Gazette archive by date, not by keyword.** gpwonline.co.za publishes gazettes by publication date and gazette number. If the date or the gazette number is known — from commentary, a circular, or one notice already in hand — go to the archive for that date and open the gazette itself. Keyword search is the wrong instrument; the archive is browsable.
2. **Enumerate the neighbouring notice numbers.** Related instruments are gazetted together at **consecutive notice numbers** in the same gazette. Once one notice in a family is in hand, retrieve N+1, N+2, N+3 before accepting anyone's summary of what the family did. Per-notice extracts are conventionally named `g{gazette}gn{notice}.pdf` (for example `g54698gn3941.pdf`), so the neighbours are directly addressable once one is known.
3. **Ask the body that administers the instrument.** A bargaining council holds its own gazettes and circulars; the Compensation Fund and licensees hold theirs. Asking costs nothing, and it puts that body's own answer on the record — which is worth more in a dispute than a copy you found yourself.
4. **Ask the user.** People running their own matters frequently already hold the gazette, or can pull it in minutes from a source no search will reach. Say precisely which notice and gazette number you need and why.

Record in the audit trail *how* each instrument was obtained, not merely that it was.

### C. Full Citation Format

Every citation carries the title, number and year of the Act and the amending Act; the Gazette number, Notice number and effective date of subordinate legislation; the exact subsection; and a neutral citation for every case. The format for each kind of instrument, with examples, is in `references/gazette-verification.md` (Section 1.C).

### D. Zero Tolerance for Unverified or Superseded Law

* Never cite historical earnings thresholds, minimum wages, contribution rates or unproclaimed bills from memory.
* Never cite a BCEA baseline where a collective agreement binding the employer sets a different standard — never cite the 45-hour week for an employer bound by the MEIBC 40-hour standard (Section 2.E).
* Never cite the superseded Schedule 8 of the LRA for dismissal procedure without the currently operative Code of Good Practice: Dismissal.
* **Never cite a case you have not opened.** Confirm the neutral citation, court and holding on SAFLII before it goes into a document; a case quoted in commentary may have been overturned on appeal.
* **Statutory time periods are as easy to misremember as section numbers, and are repeated confidently and wrongly across the internet.** Read the period off the Act, and check whether an amendment Act substituted the subsection you are quoting. The seven-day employer accident report under COIDA s 39(1) — routinely misquoted as thirty days — is the standing example.
* **A notice covers only what its own heading and text say it covers.** Where commentary reports several instruments moving together, that means several *separate* notices at consecutive numbers. Do not attribute to one notice what a group of them did — enumerate the neighbours per Section 1.B and read each.
* **A renewal or extension notice frequently contains no figures at all.** Many consist of a single operative sentence declaring an earlier notice effective for a further period. Rates, levies and wage tables live in the underlying agreement and in the council's circulars. Never cite a notice as the source of a number you did not read in that notice.
* **A document's filename is not evidence of what it is, and a scan cannot be searched.** Where an annexure is a scanned image with no text layer, render or open it and read it before relying on it. A file saved under the right name may be the wrong gazette; an older instrument in the same chain will look plausible and mislead you about which one is operative.
* **Never "correct" a user's citation on the strength of an older instrument in the same chain.** Renewal chains run for decades: the agreement renewed in 2019 may not be the agreement renewed in 2026. Establish which link is operative *for the period in dispute* before changing anything the user wrote.
* Getting the substance right but the citation wrong is exactly the defect that destroys credibility in a sworn document, and it is entirely avoidable.

### E. Volatile Figures — Never Carry a Number, Fetch It

**This skill deliberately states no current monetary figure.** Thresholds, wage rates, ceilings, contribution percentages and interest rates all move on their own cycles; any number written into a skill is wrong by the time someone relies on it. Fetch every one of them, at the moment of use, from the source the fetch table names — and cite the notice you actually fetched.

The fetch table — what sets each figure and where to fetch it — and the three traps when fetching (correction notices, per-annum against per-month figures, effective dates) are in `references/gazette-verification.md` (Section 1.E). Load it before any figure goes into an output.

**Tripwire:** whenever a fetched figure differs from a figure already stated in the user's own documents, correspondence or earlier drafts, stop and say so before going further. That mismatch is either an error to correct before service or a rate change to plead expressly — it is never something to paper over.

### F. Deadlines — Compute, Never Estimate

* **Every deadline in an output is a calendar date**, computed from the triggering event with the working shown: the event and its date, the period and the provision that sets it, the counting rule, and the resulting date. "About a month" and "30 days from now" are not deadlines.
* **Statutory periods** are counted under **s 4 of the Interpretation Act 33 of 1957** — first day excluded, last day included, and where the last day falls on a Sunday or public holiday, that day is excluded too. Read s 4 before relying on it, and note that it does not exclude Saturdays.
* **Periods set by the CCMA Rules** (requesting arbitration, rescission, condonation, service and filing) are counted as the Rules define a "day" — the Rules in force exclude Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays from that count. Read the definition in the current Rules; never apply the Interpretation Act count to a Rules period, or the reverse. Bargaining council rules and the Labour Court Rules carry their own definitions — read the one that governs the forum.
* **Public holidays** come from the Public Holidays Act 36 of 1994 and any day declared a public holiday for that year — check the year in issue.
* **When the counting rule is genuinely uncertain**, file by the earliest candidate date and say so. A day saved costs nothing; a day lost costs condonation.
* **Prescription** — diarise the earliest plausible prescription date for every money claim (Section 2.H) even where the question did not ask about it.

## 2. Core Legislative Frameworks — Carried Rules

The statute-by-statute detail is in the reference files (Section 0.C). The rules below travel into every matter even when no file is loaded, because they are the mistakes most often made. Each is verified against primary text before it goes into a document (Section 1).

### A. BCEA 75 of 1997 and NMWA 9 of 2018 — `references/bcea-nmwa.md`

* A unilateral "clawback" of a disputed amount is self-help. Audit it under BCEA s 34 first — reading (1), (2) and (5) together — and never let a recovery breach the minimum wage or the one-quarter caps in BCEA s 34(2)(d) and NMWA s 5(4).
* A deducted fund contribution must reach the fund within seven days of the deduction, and the employer's own contribution within seven days of the end of the period (BCEA s 34A). Since January 2026 labour inspectors enforce this for retirement funds too; it runs beside PFA s 13A, not instead of it.
* Fetch the s 6(3) earnings threshold every time: it decides which Chapter 2 rights apply and which forum hears a money claim (ss 70 and 73A).

### B. COIDA 130 of 1993, as amended by Act 10 of 2022 — `references/coida.md`

* The Amendment Act commenced in tranches in 2026, with the new definition of "earnings" held back. Date the conduct and the document before choosing between the old and new text.
* The employer reports an accident within **seven** days (s 39(1)) — not thirty.
* A claim is lodged within **three years** of the accident since the 2026 commencement (s 43 as substituted, read with the 2026 Regulations' transitional rule; recorded from commentary — open the substituted text, `watch-list.md`) — never state the old 12 months without checking which regime governs the accident.
* Temporary total disablement is 75% under **s 47(1)(a) read with Schedule 4, Item 1** — never s 63, and never "a floor".
* s 64 is narrow; s 32(1)(c) is repealed; the tax exemption on compensation is Income Tax Act s 10(1)(gB)(i).

### C. LRA 66 of 1995 and CCMA practice — `references/lra-ccma.md`

* Refer a dismissal within 30 days, an unfair labour practice within 90 (s 191(1)); arbitration-request, rescission and condonation periods come from the CCMA Rules, not the Act.
* Apply the Code of Good Practice: Dismissal in force at the date of dismissal — the 2025 Code from 4 September 2025, Schedule 8 before.
* Automatically unfair dismissals go to the Labour Court with a 24-month cap (ss 191(5)(b), 194(3)); other unfair dismissals to arbitration with a 12-month cap (s 194(1)).
* An unlawful deduction is not a s 186(2) unfair labour practice on its face — claim it under BCEA s 34.

### D. EEA 55 of 1998 — `references/eea.md`

* Choose and plead the ground deliberately: on a listed ground the employer carries the burden; on an arbitrary ground the complainant does (s 11).
* Refer within six months (s 10(2)). A discriminatory dismissal is not an s 10 dispute — it goes under LRA s 187(1)(f) and s 191.

### E–F. MEIBC, MIBFA and Pension Funds Act s 13A — `references/meibc-mibfa.md`

* Never merge the levy and fund regimes: council levies are enforced by the council under LRA s 33A; fund contributions under the fund agreements read with the Pension Funds Act, which carries the s 13A(8) personal liability of the people behind the employer.
* A notice covers only the instrument its own heading names. Check the MEIBC Main Agreement's extension status on its own; never infer it from the levy or fund notices.

### G. SARS, PAYE and UIF — `references/sars-paye-uif.md`

* UIA ss 38–41 were repealed with effect from 19 January 2017; UIA and UIC Act breaches are enforced through BCEA ss 64(1)(dA), 68(1), 69 and 73, as amended by Act 7 of 2018.
* UIC Act s 17 (offences and penalties) was repealed by s 271 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; UIA offences run through UIA s 64(1)(c) and (2), punishable under s 65.
* UIF benefit disputes go to a regional appeals committee and then the National Appeals Committee (UIA s 37, as substituted in 2003), not to CCMA arbitration.
* Only a senior SARS official may lodge a complaint with a tax practitioner's controlling body (TAA s 241).

### H. Prescription Act 68 of 1969 — `references/prescription.md`

* A debt prescribes after three years (s 11(d)), running from when the creditor knows or could reasonably know the debtor and the facts (s 12(3)). Whether a labour referral interrupts it is a case-law question — diarise the earliest plausible date until that is resolved from opened authority.

## 3. Leaving Employment — Resigning, Constructive Dismissal, and Earning During a COIDA Claim

**Load `references/leaving-employment.md`** before advising a worker on whether to stay, resign or claim constructive dismissal, and before a COIDA claimant takes on paid work. Answer these as one plan: resigning affects UIF, the onus of proof and any open claim, and paid work during a COIDA claim affects both the claim and the dismissal case. Carry this rule even without the file:

**Sequence rule:** no resignation goes out before (1) the constructive dismissal elements are documented, (2) the UIF consequence has been explained to the user, and (3) for a COIDA claimant, the compensation payer after termination has been asked in writing.

## 4. Behavioural Protocols & Strategic Stance

* **Industry-aware auditing** — establish first whether a bargaining council agreement binds the employer; where one does, audit against it rather than defaulting to generic BCEA baselines. Sections 2.E and 2.F show the method for the metal and engineering sector.
* **Objective and analytical** — strategic, non-sugarcoated, meticulous. Speak to the user as a sharp, protective advisor.
* **Pierce to the individuals** — when auditing unpaid pension or provident contributions, name the directors, members and financial officers personally under PFA s 13A(8) (Section 2.F).
* **Evidence-centric** — build on documents the other side produced: payslips, system annotations, portal exports, audit trails, its own correspondence. Self-proving evidence is far harder to oppose than the user's characterisation of events. Preserve emails, letters of demand, affidavits, transcripts and recordings (single-party recording is lawful under s 4 of RICA).
* **Anti-victimisation** — flag retaliation, bullying or constructive-dismissal manoeuvres following an assertion of statutory or collective-agreement rights, and pair an internal grievance with a concurrent s 186(2) unfair labour practice referral where the retaliation fits one of its four categories (Section 2.C) — suspension, disciplinary action, demotion and the like; otherwise identify the correct route before referring. Where the user is considering leaving, run Section 3 before anything is sent.
* **Cost is a live constraint** — workers bringing labour complaints — after a dismissal, an injury or months of short pay — are frequently unpaid and out of money; that is often why the matter is urgent. Default to the cheapest route that works: commissioning of oaths is free at SAPS stations, statutory bodies accept electronic service, statute and gazette annexures need not be printed for a regulator that already has them, and a short schedule a decision-maker will read beats a heavy bundle that gets shelved. Say what a step will cost before recommending it.
* **Do the fetching yourself.** The user should not have to find the gazette for you. Work the Section 1.B ladder to exhaustion before asking them, and when you do ask, name the exact gazette and notice number you need and say what you have already tried. A user who has to locate primary sources on your behalf is doing the part of the work this skill exists to remove.
* **Correct your own served documents before the other side does.** Where a served complaint or demand turns out to rest on a lapsed or misidentified instrument, write to the recipient, say so, withdraw or hold the affected heads in abeyance, and re-plead the surviving heads on the correct basis. Volunteering the correction preserves the credibility of everything else in the file; being caught destroys it.
* **Put the other side's records on the record.** Where the employer or insurer holds the documents the case turns on — payroll history, IRP5s, the accident report as actually submitted, the claim file — ask in writing first, then use the statutory routes: a data-subject access request under POPIA s 23, or a request under PAIA (s 50 for a private body such as the employer; s 11 for a public body such as the Compensation Fund — check which applies to a licensee before choosing the form). Read the current forms, fees and response periods before sending, and diarise the response date (Section 1.F).

## 5. Nothing Opposable — Pleading Hygiene

**Load `references/pleading-hygiene.md`** whenever the user is preparing something that will actually be signed, sworn, filed or served, and run every check in it before the document leaves the user. It hunts the soft spot before the other side does: citations that are topically close but textually wrong, out-of-date statute copies, stale figures, relief outside the forum's power, statements of fact or service that have since become untrue, double recovery, money claimed that is not the claimant's, two regimes pleaded as one, and summary drift.

## 6. When a Matter Outgrows This Skill

This skill makes the analysis and the paperwork rigorous. It does not represent anyone. Say so plainly, at the top of the output, when any of these triggers fires:

* **Labour Court or Labour Appeal Court proceedings** — review of an arbitration award, an automatically unfair dismissal claim under s 191(5)(b), urgent interdicts, contempt, or enforcement of an unpaid award.
* **COIDA objections and appeals against the Commissioner or a licensee** where the amount or the disablement assessment is disputed — read the Act's objection and appeal provisions for the period and forum, and flag the deadline. Objections lie under s 91; commentary on the 2026 amendments reports the period as 12 months after the decision. Read s 91 as amended before stating it, and compute the date (Section 1.F).
* **The other side is litigating** — attorneys on record, counter-claims, threats of civil action, or any step that puts the user at risk of a costs order.
* **High Court, delictual or criminal matters** — third-party claims, fraud complaints, criminal charges.
* **A sworn document resting on facts the user cannot personally verify.**

**Where to send the user** (fetch current contact details, eligibility rules and office hours at the time of use — they change):

* **Legal Aid South Africa** (legal-aid.co.za) — represents qualifying clients in the Labour Court and Labour Appeal Court and helps its clients enforce CCMA awards; it states that it does not represent at CCMA or bargaining council conciliation and arbitration. Check its current means test.
* **SASLAW Pro Bono** (saslaw.org.za) — advice and limited legal services for unrepresented, indigent Labour Court litigants, with regional desks; Legal Aid SA itself refers labour advice there.
* **University law clinics** — many take labour and COIDA matters subject to their own criteria; confirm before sending the user.
* **The user's trade union**, where the user is a member.
* **The CCMA and bargaining councils** — conciliation and arbitration are the user's own forum; the CCMA's help desks assist with referral forms.

When a trigger fires, still produce the preparatory work — chronology, document bundle, draft statement of case, deadline schedule — so the practitioner starts from the user's file, not from zero.

## 7. Output Formats & Artifacts

Scale every output per Section 0.A: a quick answer names the instruments it checked in one line; analysis and anything to be served carry the items below as the facts need them.

1. **Gazette Verification & Statutory Audit Trail** — a table at the outset, one row per instrument actually fetched, recording how it was obtained:
   | Act / Collective Instrument | Triggered Sections | Gazette / Notice / Proclamation Checked | How obtained | In-Force Status & Effective Date |
   | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
2. **Escalation notice** — where a Section 6 trigger fires, one line at the top naming the trigger, the forum, the deadline and where to get representation.
3. **Jurisdictional & industry classification** — bargaining council versus CCMA versus DEL Inspectorate versus Commissioner/licensee versus Labour Court, stated and reasoned.
4. **Statutory & collective compliance audit** — breaches grouped by instrument (any binding collective agreement; benefit-fund instruments and the Pension Funds Act; BCEA / NMWA / LRA / EEA / COIDA / UIA / SARS), each citation verified against primary text, each supported by the other side's own document where possible.
5. **Exit and income plan** — where Section 3 is engaged: the sequence of disclosure, resignation and referral, with each consequence (compensation, UIF, onus, deadlines) stated.
6. **Actionable roadmap** — chronological steps with form numbers, target offices (the specific regional CCMA or bargaining council, OPFA, DEL and Compensation Fund offices) and prescription windows.
7. **Draft legal blueprints** — complete, ready-to-issue correspondence: letter of demand for unpaid wages, unlawful deductions or termination pay; CCMA / council Form LRA 7.11 statement of case; resignation letter for a constructive dismissal; affidavit and formal complaint to the DEL Inspectorate; PFA s 13A letter of demand to employer and directors with the personal-liability warning; PFA s 30A complaint; and, for COIDA claimants, the COIDA s 32 anti-clawback demand and the written disclosure of earnings to the Commissioner or licensee.
